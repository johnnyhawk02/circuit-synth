Moog modular patch, solved at component level

A step sequencer plays a discrete saw-core VCO into the Moog 904A transistor-ladder filter and the 902 VCA, both shaped by a 911 envelope. Every transistor, diode, JFET, resistor and capacitor from the original Moog schematics is solved live in your browser (modified nodal analysis + Newton), no DSP shortcuts.

What is simulated, and what was assumed
  • 904A (drawing 1149): input buffer Q22/Q23, adder (as an op-amp macro), Q6 exponential converter, ladder Q7–Q16 on the 680/220/220/220/68 Ω bias chain with 1.2/0.3/0.075 µF range caps, output buffer Q17–Q21. The scan's regeneration connection would not resonate as drawn, so it is taken from the opposite output phase (Q20).
  • 902 (fig. 9, EXP mode): adder, Q10 expo, Q6/Q7 input pair, Q8/Q9 gain pair, Q11/Q12 PNP output with Q13 current source. OUTPUT BIAS is trimmed to 0 V at start-up, as in the alignment procedure.
  • 911 (fig. 17): S-trigger switch, Q1 attack / Q3 release / Q2 decay switches, Q8 sustain follower, Q11–Q14 flip-flop, Q9/Q10 comparator, Q5 2N4303 JFET follower. Solved at 6 kHz; flip-flop transistors get 1 nF junction capacitance so their flips can be sub-stepped.
  • VCO: not a Moog module. A textbook saw core: matched expo pair with an op-amp reference, op-amp integrator, Schmitt comparator and JFET reset.
  • Modules are joined by ideal voltage ports (patch cables), and the sequencer is digital.
